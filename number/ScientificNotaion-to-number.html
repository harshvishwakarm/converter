Scientific Notation

Formula:

Scientific Notation: a × 10^n (where 1 ≤ |a| < 10 and n is an integer)

Examples:

  • 123000 → 1.23 × 10⁵
  • 0.00456 → 4.56 × 10⁻³
  • 9876543 → 9.876543 × 10⁶
  • 0.000000789 → 7.89 × 10⁻⁷

About Scientific Notation

Scientific notation is a way of expressing numbers that are too large or too small to be conveniently written in decimal form. It's widely used in science, engineering, and mathematics to represent very large numbers (like distances between stars) or very small numbers (like the size of atoms). The format is a × 10^n, where 'a' is a number between 1 and 10, and 'n' is the power of 10.

When to Use Scientific Notation

Scientific notation is particularly useful for:

  • Very large numbers: 300,000,000 = 3.0 × 10⁸ (speed of light in m/s)
  • Very small numbers: 0.00000000016 = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ (size of an atom in meters)
  • Calculations involving powers of 10
  • Scientific research and data analysis
  • Engineering calculations

FAQs

Q: How do I convert scientific notation back to regular numbers?

A: Use the swap button and enter notation like "1.23e5" or "1.23 × 10⁵".

Q: What's the difference between E notation and × 10 notation?

A: They're the same: 1.23E5 = 1.23 × 10⁵ = 123000. E notation is used in calculators and computers.

Q: Can I enter negative numbers?

A: Yes, negative numbers work the same way: -123000 = -1.23 × 10⁵.
